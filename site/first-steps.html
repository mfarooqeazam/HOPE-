Not sure where to start?

Most families arrive without knowing what to ask for. Tell us what you are noticing and this page will show which services usually fit, what an assessment involves, and what happens next.

Read this first. This is a guide to our services, not a test and not a diagnosis. It cannot tell you whether your child has a condition, and nothing here is scored. Only an assessment with a qualified clinician can answer that. Your answers stay in your browser — nothing is sent anywhere unless you choose to contact us afterwards.

Before you worry

Three things worth hearing first

Being unsure is normal

Almost nobody arrives knowing what to ask for. Parents are not supposed to be able to tell the difference between a late talker and a language disorder — that is our job, not yours.

You are not overreacting

“Wait and see” is the most common advice families are given, and it is the reason most children are identified years later than they could have been. Asking early costs a conversation.

Nothing here is a verdict

A concern is a starting point, not a diagnosis. Plenty of children we assess need no ongoing therapy at all, and we will tell you so rather than sell you something.

What to bring

Come with whatever you have — including nothing

You do not need to prepare. If you would like to, these are the things that genuinely help us.

  • What you have noticed, in your own words. A few notes on your phone are enough. When it happens, how often, and what usually comes before it.
  • A video, if there is one. Thirty seconds of an ordinary moment at home tells us more than an hour of a child being observed by a stranger.
  • Any reports you already have — school, hospital, a previous therapist. Bring them to the appointment; please do not send them through this website.
  • What a good outcome would look like to you. Not a clinical goal — a real one. Sitting through a meal. Being invited back. Sleeping.
  • Your questions. Especially the ones you feel silly asking. Those are usually the ones nobody has answered.

Ask us anything

Questions you are entitled to ask — of us, or of anyone

If you are comparing centres, these are the questions worth asking. We have answered them for ourselves; a good provider will answer them too, and a poor one will change the subject.

  • Who exactly will work with my child, and what are they qualified in? You should get a name and a credential, not “our expert team”.
  • Can I see the credential? Ours is published with its number and dates on the About page, so you can check it rather than take our word for it.
  • What are the goals, and how will we know if they are met? A plan without written goals and a way of measuring them is not a plan.
  • When would you expect to stop? Nobody can give an exact date, but anyone unwilling to discuss an end point at all is worth being careful with.
  • What will you teach me to do at home? If the answer is nothing, most of the week is being wasted.
  • What does it cost, and what happens if I cannot keep paying? Ask before you start, not after. Here is our answer.

If this is urgent

When not to wait for a form

This site is not an emergency service and nobody monitors it overnight.

If someone is in immediate danger

Contact emergency services or go to your nearest hospital. That includes a child or adult at risk of serious harm to themselves or to someone else.

If you want to track development properly

Use a real checklist, free

We deliberately do not publish our own milestone checklist. Writing one would mean inventing clinical content, and there is already a good free one used worldwide.

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention publishes Learn the Signs. Act Early. — milestone checklists from 2 months to 5 years, revised in 2022 so that each milestone is one most children (75% or more) reach by that age. It is free, it is available in several languages, and there is a Milestone Tracker app. It is designed to start a conversation with a professional, not to give a diagnosis.

When you are ready

You do not have to have it worked out first
